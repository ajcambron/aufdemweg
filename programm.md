# Programm & Ablauf

How a Sunday runs, for the regular instructor or a sub. Every week follows this pattern, so once you've read this page, any week's page makes sense.

## The class

High school students, all in one room, ranging from true beginners (A1) to students who can hold a real conversation (B2, a few near C1). There is no separate beginner group. Everyone works on the same theme and the same task; what changes by level is how much support each student gets.

We use **du** with each other in class.

## The three rules

**1. Every week builds to one task.** The *Kernaufgabe* (core task) is the point of the day: an interview, a survey, a draw-what-you-hear exercise. Vocabulary and grammar are only there because the task needs them. If the day goes sideways, protect the Kernaufgabe and let the rest go.

**2. Input before output.** Students read and listen first, and answer questions about what they read, before they're asked to say or write anything with the new language. Don't open with "repeat after me" or "make a sentence with *meinen*." The packet is already in this order; follow it top to bottom.

**3. You are a resource, not the source.** Students should get information from each other, from the texts, and from the cards in the packet. Your job is to set up the activity, keep time, circulate, and answer questions when asked. A class where you're talking for most of two hours has gone wrong, even if it felt productive.

## The packet model

The packet holds about 150 minutes of material for a 120-minute class, on purpose. Nobody runs out, and the optional modules can be dropped without breaking anything.

| Modul | Min | | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Einstieg (warm-up) | 10 | Core | Picture talk, a quick poll, or movement. Whole class together. |
| Input & structured input | 20 | Core | Sentence builder, then two short readings at two levels, then questions about them. Students *answer*; they don't produce the new grammar yet. |
| **Kernaufgabe** | 30 | Core | The week's task. Partner or group work with an information gap: each person has something the other needs. |
| Structured output | 20 | Core | Now students produce. A1/A2 fill in sentence frames; B1/B2 write or speak more freely, and a partner has to respond to what they said. |
| Können-Sie...? exit ticket | 5 | Core | A two-line self-check at the student's own level. |
| Landeskunde | 15 | Optional | A short culture note tied to the theme. Good read-aloud-and-discuss. |
| Vokabelspiel / energizer | 15 | Optional | A game. First thing to cut. |
| B1/B2 extension | 20 | Optional | A harder text for stronger students. Best run as a station while others finish structured output. |
| Buffer | 15 | Optional | Instructor's choice. |

**If you're behind,** cut in this order: energizer, buffer, Landeskunde. Never cut the Kernaufgabe.

## Working with mixed levels

Every module has the same task for everyone, with different support:

- **A1/A2:** use the question banks and sentence frames as printed. Present tense is fine. Accept short answers. A student who answers *"Vierzehn."* to *"Wie alt bist du?"* has communicated.
- **B1/B2:** push for full sentences, a reason (*weil ...*), a follow-up question. These students are your best resource for pairing: put a B1 with an A1 for the Kernaufgabe and the B1 becomes the input.

Correct gently and mostly by restating. If a student says *"Ich habe fünfzehn,"* answer *"Ah, du bist fünfzehn!"* and move on. Save explicit correction for the structured output module, and even then, respond to what they said before how they said it.

## Using a week page

Each week page has the same sections:

1. **Auf einen Blick:** the can-do goals and the Kernaufgabe in one line.
2. **Ablauf:** the module table for that week, plus materials.
3. **Grammatik:** a refresher for *you*: the rule, the mistake students will make, and what to say about it. Students get at most a minute of this, in context.
4. **Wortschatz:** the week's vocabulary with meanings.
5. **Arbeitsblatt:** the student packet. Print one per student.
6. **Lösungen:** the answer key, folded so it doesn't spoil the page.
