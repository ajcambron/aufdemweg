## Auf einen Blick

| A1 can-do | B1/B2 can-do |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introduce myself and answer basic personal questions | Discuss my background and interests in some detail |

**Kernaufgabe:** Klassen-Bingo. Students mingle and ask each other questions from a bank; when someone answers yes, their name goes in the box. Each name can be used only twice, which forces them to talk to more people. B1/B2 ask one follow-up and write down an extra detail.

{: .important }
This week doubles as **informal placement.** While students mingle, note who answers fluently, who needs the question read aloud, who produces *weil*-clauses without being asked, and who goes quiet and seems to translate in their head. Those notes set your pairings for Weeks 2–5.

## Grammatik

**A1: no new grammar.** Students reuse two verbs as fixed phrases: *ich heiße / du heißt* and *ich bin / du bist*. Don't conjugate them on the board; the sentence builder already has every form they need.

**B1/B2: *weil* sends the verb to the end.**

> Ich lerne Deutsch, **weil** meine Familie aus Deutschland **kommt**.
> Ich lerne Deutsch, **weil** ich nach Deutschland reisen **möchte**.

In a *weil*-clause, the conjugated verb (the one that changes for *ich/du/er*) moves to the very end. With a helper verb like *möchte*, the helper goes last and the infinitive sits just before it.

{: .warning }
**What students will do:** keep English word order, so you hear *"weil ich möchte reisen nach Deutschland."* The meaning comes through fine, so in conversation, restate it correctly and move on: *"Ah, weil du nach Deutschland reisen möchtest!"* Point out the rule only in structured output, where B1/B2 are writing *weil*-sentences on purpose.

**Du oder Sie:** *du* is for friends, family and classmates; *Sie* is for adults you don't know, teachers and officials. In this class, everyone uses *du*.
